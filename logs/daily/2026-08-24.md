## 2026-08-24: Decision: Normalising Clips to Frame Rate vs adjusting Wav2Lip clip-to-clip

**Decision: Normalise everything to 25fps**

Reasoning: Adjusting Wav2Lips expected rate per clip isn't really viable, 
SyncNets contrastive loss assumes a 5-video-frame : 1-audio-window (0.2s)
relationship at 25fps/16kHz. If fps needs to vary per clip, then I need to
vary the audio window size per clip to keep that ratio, which is out of scope.
This would also make the LSE-D/LSE-C metric non-comparable between clips.

The biggest risk with this approach is that a lot of Youtube-sourced mp4s 
vary between frame rates, not just a different constant fps. A naive -r 25
re-encode on this type of source can duplicate or drop frames inconsistently
and de-sync audio. I will need to force constant frame rate output.

## 2026-08-24: Normalising Clips is Unnecessary

After writing a shell script to check the current fps of my clips, I found
that they all returned 25fps, and not a single one was different. To double
check this, I wrote a verification script that checks specific clips fps 
to account for varying fps in a clip, and all of my sample tests passed. This
means that normalising to 25fps is unnecessary, however if I add more clips 
to my training/test split, I will need to re-check fps.
